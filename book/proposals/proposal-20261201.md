## What counts as a result here

- A change to a model that someone could make (a scale, a head, a set of frequencies, a
  training schedule, a layout of layers), with the reason for it stated in this repository's
  terms: slope, nearness, coverage, averages, one factor per step.
- At least one piece of the reason proved in Lean here.
- A measurement with the prediction and the stop rule written and pushed before the run, as
  [CLAUDE.md](../../CLAUDE.md) says. The measurement must be able to fail.
- A paragraph that says what the result does **not** say. The proofs are bounds for one piece
  of a model at a time. None of them says why a real model fails at a million tokens, and no
  text may read as if it did.

## Marks used below

- **proved**: a theorem in `lean/SquigglyLine/` with that name exists today.
- **to prove**: a statement I believe is true and short to prove. Not in Lean.
- **checked by hand**: derived on paper and checked with numbers on 25 Nov 2026. Not in Lean.
- **my reading**: an interpretation of mine. Not derived.
- **recalled**: from memory of the literature. Not checked against any source. Check each one

## The five ideas, most usable first

### 1. Make attention sharper as the context grows, by a formula

- **The trouble.** With scores bounded by `B`, every token gets at least `exp(-2B) / n` of the
  attention (**proved**: `every_token_gets_a_share` in
  `Supporting/NetworkShape/AttentionRate.lean`). The mirror is that no token gets more than
  `1 / (1 + (n - 1) exp(-2B))`, which is at most `exp(2B) / n` (**to prove**; **checked by
  hand**). So with bounded scores the one token that matters fades like `1 / n`.
- **The change.** Multiply the scores by a factor that grows like `log n`. If the needle's
  score is `gap` above the scores of all the other tokens, and those are equal, its share is
  `s` exactly when `gap = log(n - 1) + log(s / (1 - s))` (**checked by hand**).
- **The price.** A larger gap between scores at nearby queries is a steeper score function.
  Chapter 28 says what a steeper function costs: the output moves more for a small change of
  the input (**proved** there for networks in general; the statement for this case is **to
  prove**).
- **A side effect to check.** `depth_merges_the_tokens_at_a_geometric_rate` has the shrink
  factor `1 - exp(-2B)` per layer. A larger `B` brings that factor nearer to one, so sharper
  attention should also merge the tokens more slowly (**my reading** of a **proved** bound;
  the bound is an upper bound, so this is a guess about the true rate).
- **Known already.** "Scalable softmax" scales the scores with `log n` (**recalled**).

### 2. Give a counting head a sum, or the length as an input

- **The trouble.** Attention returns an average (**proved**:
  `attention_output_lies_among_the_values`). With `k` marked tokens out of `n`, the average
  is `k / n`, so the counts `k` and `k + 1` are `1 / n` apart in what the head hands on. A
  read-out that tells them apart needs slope at least `n` (**proved** in general:
  `sharp_count_costs_slope` in `Supporting/NewInputs/Counting.lean`; the `1 / n` step is
  **to prove**, **checked by hand**).
- **The change.** A head that adds instead of averaging, or a model that receives `n` (or
  `log n`) as an input. Then the read-out needs slope 1 at any length.
- **The harder version.** Whether the count is odd is the zigzag of chapter 49: a shallow
  reader of the count needs one unit per token less one (**proved**:
  `shallow_network_needs_one_unit_per_bit_less_one`), and a deep narrow one needs about
  `log n` layers (**proved**: `deep_narrow_network_computes_parity`).
- **Known already.** That transformers find parity and counting hard is well known (Hahn
  2020 and later work, **recalled**).

### 3. Choose position frequencies from a bound

- **The object.** The overlap of two positions is a sum of cosines of their gap (**proved**:
  `position_overlap_equals_sum_of_cosines_of_the_gap` in `Networks/Positions.lean`), and a
  rotary score depends only on the gap (**proved**: `rotary_score_depends_on_position_gap`).
  As a function of the gap this is a squiggly line, and it is the same object as the two
  prime wave of chapter 48.
- **Near repeats.** With `k` frequencies and `L + 1` positions, if `L + 1` is more than
  `(1 / ε) ^ k`, two positions have every phase within `ε` of a turn of each other, so some
  gap of at most `L` looks almost like gap zero (**to prove**, by the pigeonhole principle;
  **checked by hand**). Avoiding that needs at least `log(L + 1) / log(1 / ε)` frequencies.
  Frequencies with no whole-number relation, such as logarithms of primes, never repeat
  exactly (**proved** for two: `ratio_of_prime_logs_is_irrational`), but they still come
  near (**proved** for two, with a fixed reach:
  `two_prime_clocks_reach_every_pair_of_phases_within_a_fixed_distance`).
- **What this bound is not.** Real position codes have hundreds of frequencies, far more than
  `log L`, so this count is not what limits them (**my reading**). The measurement below is
  there to find out whether near repeats happen at all in the standard codes.
- **Coverage of each clock.** A frequency `ω` has gone once round its circle within a
  training length `L_train` only if `ω × L_train` is at least `2π`. The slow frequencies
  have not, so at longer gaps they show the model phases it never saw. Those are new inputs
  in the sense of Part VII. The notion in the library is `COVERS_WITHIN` (**my reading**; the
  statement for position codes is **to prove**).
- **Known already.** YaRN rescales the slow rotary frequencies for longer contexts, and gave
  the kernel and Fourier feature theory of chapters 10 and 11 as its motive (**recalled**).

### 4. Give far links the steps they need

- **The trouble.** Chapter 18: along each direction the error shrinks by one factor per
  step, and the weak directions go last (**proved** for the linear model:
  `residual_along_the_path_is_a_share_of_the_start` in `Networks/LinearModel.lean` and the
  files behind chapter 18). A link between far tokens is rare in the data, so it is a weak
  direction (**my reading**; this step is not derived and is the first thing to check).
- **The change.** Weight the examples with far links more, or train longer on long
  sequences, by an amount read from the strength of the direction instead of by trial.
- **Risk.** This is the least certain of the five. If "rare" does not turn into "small
  eigenvalue" in a way that can be written down, drop it.

### 5. Read far with depth

- **The trouble.** A stack of local windows sees `layer_count × (window - 1)` tokens and no
  more (**proved**: `convolution_stack_reads_only_its_exact_window` in
  `Supporting/NetworkShape/Convolution.lean`). To see `L` tokens it needs about
  `L / (window - 1)` layers.
- **The change.** Let the window double with each layer. Then `layer_count` layers see about
  `2 ^ layer_count` tokens, the same doubling as the tent layers of chapter 8 (**to prove**;
  read first what `Convolution.lean` already has for stride).
- **Known already.** Dilated convolutions and sparse attention do this (**recalled**).

## Explanations that are not designs

Two more results say why some long-context models must lose something. They suggest no
change, but they belong in the same chapters.

- **A memory of fixed size must confuse two contexts.** `memorising_needs_one_bit_per_stored_bit`
  and `bottleneck` (**proved**) apply to anything that squeezes the context into a fixed
  state: recurrent models, state space models, summaries, compressed caches. The statement
  for a sequence of tokens is **to prove**.
- **A recurrent memory fades as a product of gates.**
  `memory_gap_after_many_steps_is_product_of_gates` (**proved**). To remember across `n`
  steps the gates must be within about `1 / n` of one (**to prove**).

## First measurement

Ideas 1 and 2 together, on one small transformer that fits the GPU of this machine.

- Train on short contexts and test on longer ones, on two tasks: find the needle, and count a
  marked token.
- Compare four models: unchanged; scores scaled with `log n`; a head that sums; both changes.
- Draft predictions. The binding ones go into the notebook and are pushed before the run.
  - Unchanged: the needle's share of the attention falls like `1 / n` beyond the trained
    length, and counting fails beyond the trained length.
  - With the scale: the share stays within a stated band of its value at the trained length.
  - With the sum: the counting error at the longest length is within a stated band of the
    error at the trained length.
- This can fail. The bounds are for one attention layer. A full model has feed-forward
  layers and skips and may route round them, in either direction: the unchanged model may do
  better than the bound suggests, or the changed one may do no better.
- A second, cheaper measurement for idea 3 needs no training: for the standard sinusoidal
  code and for rotary frequencies, find the smallest gap beyond a stated minimum at which the
  overlap comes back within `ε` of its largest value, and compare it with the pigeonhole
  count.
